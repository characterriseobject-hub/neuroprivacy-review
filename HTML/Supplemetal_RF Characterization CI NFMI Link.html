Supplementary Methods

S1.1 Boundary-condition framework

The Formalized Boundary-Condition Advisor distinguishes three evidentiary tiers: established mechanics (Maxwell–Faraday induction, sampling theory, FFT, Manchester line coding, SHA-256 integrity), open-frontier corridors (the specific carrier, packet structure, telemetry behavior, and security posture of a given device), and reported-only claims (any assertion about implantation, consent, operator identity, or causation)[reference:0]. The workflow below is designed so that a null or negative result is publishable and is never overwritten by narrative inference. This mirrors the advisory posture of the 2017 ShmooCon work, which treated physical-layer findings as provisional and device-specific[reference:1].

S1.2 Hosts, receiver, and capture environment

Two hosts are identified before any receiver action: the Codex host (analysis environment) and the SDR host (receiver-connected machine). The active SDR application, receiver model, USB descriptor, and capture folder are recorded. If the receiver model or serial is not exposed by the application, the field is marked UNKNOWN in the metadata sidecar rather than inferred. An in-progress recording session is left intact; no application is restarted or reconfigured during capture.

Materials: macOS hardware/OS query — Terminal, system_profiler SPUSBDataType; Windows device enumeration — Get-PnpDevice; RTL-SDR test utility — rtl_test.

S1.3 NFMI physical-layer baseline and target characterization

Cochlear-implant transcutaneous links are overwhelmingly inductive near-field links. A 2026 physics-based design-space analysis in Scientific Reports characterizes a 12-coil library at the ISM bands 6.78, 13.56, and 27.12 MHz and notes that clinical devices typically operate at 13.56 MHz, with the coils held in alignment by a retention magnet[reference:2][reference:3]. A 2025 AUDITORY list exchange records Advanced Bionics processors at 49 MHz in some generations, with the transmitter band falling in an unlicensed fixed/mobile allocation adjacent to a ham band[reference:4]. A patent literature range places the magnetic-induction link between 148.5 kHz and 30 MHz, typically 3–15 MHz[reference:5]. These values are the anchored baseline; the specific device under test is treated as UNKNOWN until measured.

The 2017 task reported Manchester encoding, 12-bit words, 300-word standard packets, a plaintext forward link, and no non-volatile RAM in the implanted receiver[reference:6]. These are historical findings from one Advanced Bionics HiRes 90K device and are not generalized. Current devices may differ in carrier, framing, encryption state, and telemetry asymmetry. The UmboMic PVDF cantilever microphone program is noted as a future totally-implantable sensor architecture, not evidence of a deployed bidirectional neural interface: its reported equivalent input noise is 32.3 dB SPL over 100 Hz–7 kHz, with a flat response within 10 dB to 6 kHz, and it is explicitly a proof-of-concept for a fully internal cochlear implant[reference:7][reference:8].

Boundary note. Published capabilities and connected conceptual boxes do not establish an integrated system or its use in an individual. The UmboMic is a sensor, not a stimulator or a telemetry transceiver; its specifications are cited only to bound what is physically plausible for a middle-ear microphone, not to imply that any particular person carries one.

S1.4 Receive chain and antenna

Two receiver classes are used. For discovery, a Nooelec NESDR SMArTee XTR (~65 MHz–2.3 GHz, 0.5 ppm TCXO) or RTL-SDR Blog V4 provides spectrum and waterfall visibility; because several CI carriers sit below 65 MHz, a Nooelec Ham It Up upconverter or an SDRplay RSPdx is used for the 10–60 MHz region. For waveform fidelity, a digital oscilloscope with CSV export (≥20 MS/s, ≥100 MHz analog bandwidth) and a shielded loop or ferrite-rod probe is used. The antenna is a small multi-turn air-core or shielded loop held adjacent to the external transmitter coil; a broadband whip is not appropriate for a localized near-field magnetic signal.

Materials: Nooelec NESDR SMArTee XTR; Nooelec Ham It Up; RTL-SDR Blog; SDRplay RSPdx.

S1.5 Reference capture and metadata sidecar

The reference capture is 5–10 s of raw complex I/Q from a known local FM broadcast station, recorded at a published center frequency (e.g., 98.7 MHz) with a sample rate ≥2.4 MS/s and fixed gain (AGC off). The FM signal has a known absolute frequency and a recognizable spectral shape; it therefore catches frequency-axis, FFT-scaling, and waterfall-time-axis errors before they contaminate a weaker NFMI capture. The implant-link capture is a separate later step and is never conflated with the reference validation.

Metadata are stored in SigMF format: a .sigmf-data file holding interleaved samples and a .sigmf-meta JSON sidecar with global, captures, and annotations objects[reference:9]. Required fields are core:datatype (e.g., cf32_le, ci16_le, cu8), core:sample_rate, core:frequency, UTC time, receiver model, application, sample format and I/Q order, and gain/AGC state. Unknown fields are explicitly marked UNKNOWN. The SigMF schema is published at sigmf.org, with a Python implementation at sigmf/sigmf-python[reference:10].

S1.6 Hash-based chain of custody

The original capture is hashed on the SDR host before any copy or conversion. macOS uses shasum -a 256 capture.sigmf-data; Windows uses Get-FileHash .\capture.sigmf-data -Algorithm SHA256. The copied file is verified against the digest on the analysis host. A matching hash proves byte-level integrity of the copy; it does not prove that the original capture is authentic, uncorrupted at the receiver, or attributable to a particular device. SigMF additionally supports a SHA-512 hash of the data file in the global metadata object[reference:11]; both SHA-256 and SHA-512 hashes are recorded in our sidecar for redundancy.

S1.7 Spectrum and waterfall analysis

The analysis script (Supplementary Code S2.1) produces a centered power spectrum via a Welch-like average (Hann window, 50% overlap, NFFT = 4096 unless otherwise specified) and a spectrogram waterfall with an absolute RF frequency axis and elapsed time. Level units are relative dB and are labeled uncalibrated. If the export contains demodulated audio rather than raw I/Q, an audio-frequency plot is produced and labeled accordingly; the two data types are never merged in a single figure without explicit axis labeling.

S1.8 Manchester / frame decoding

For the NFMI link, the decoding chain is envelope detection → threshold → Manchester decode → word/frame segmentation. Three tools are used in analysis-only mode: Universal Radio Hacker (URH) 2.10.x, which supports Manchester I/II line-code decoding and protocol analysis[reference:12]; the URH-NG AI fork, which adds automated modulation and line-code classification but requires manual confirmation of every result[reference:13]; and inspectrum for visual symbol-rate and transition analysis[reference:14]. A custom Python Manchester decoder is used for the final audit, because a short, auditable script is preferable to a GUI tool when the protocol is fixed. URH and similar tools are disconnected from any transmit-capable radio during decoding.

S1.9 Synthetic validation protocol

Before any live capture is trusted, the pipeline is validated against generated complex I/Q containing a tone 50 kHz above center and a second tone 75 kHz below center, 6 dB weaker. The sample rate is 2.4 MS/s, which contains both. Pass criteria are: correct frequency signs; peak locations within one FFT bin (bin width = fs/NFFT ≈ 586 Hz at 2.4 MS/s and NFFT = 4096); and relative peak level within 1 dB. The synthetic test is reported separately from the live receiver check. If the synthetic test fails, the pipeline is fixed before live data are analyzed.

S1.10 Live receiver check separation

The live receiver check is documented in its own section of the findings report, with its own capture identifier, hash, and metadata sidecar. It is never merged with the synthetic validation table. The findings report distinguishes established by measurement, inferred, and unknown / requires independent confirmation.

Supplementary Code

S2.1 analyze_iq.py — spectrum and waterfall

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
analyze_iq.py — receive-only spectrum + waterfall for raw complex I/Q.
Usage:
  python analyze_iq.py capture.sigmf-data --fs 2400000 --fc 98700000 --fmt cf32 --nfft 4096 --out plots/

Dependencies: numpy, matplotlib
License: MIT (supplementary code for peer review)
"""
import argparse, json, os, sys
import numpy as np
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def load_iq(path, fmt):
    if fmt == "cf32":
        return np.fromfile(path, dtype=np.complex64)
    if fmt == "ci16":
        raw = np.fromfile(path, dtype=np.int16)
        return (raw[0::2] + 1j*raw[1::2]).astype(np.complex64) / 32768.0
    if fmt == "cu8":
        raw = np.fromfile(path, dtype=np.uint8)
        return ((raw[0::2].astype(np.float32)-127.5) +
                1j*(raw[1::2].astype(np.float32)-127.5)) / 128.0
    raise ValueError("fmt must be cf32, ci16, or cu8")

def main():
    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    ap.add_argument("data")
    ap.add_argument("--fs", type=float, required=True, help="sample rate, I/Q pairs/s")
    ap.add_argument("--fc", type=float, required=True, help="center frequency, Hz")
    ap.add_argument("--fmt", default="cf32", choices=["cf32","ci16","cu8"])
    ap.add_argument("--nfft", type=int, default=4096)
    ap.add_argument("--overlap", type=float, default=0.5)
    ap.add_argument("--out", default="plots")
    args = ap.parse_args()

    os.makedirs(args.out, exist_ok=True)
    x = load_iq(args.data, args.fmt)
    if x.size == 0:
        sys.exit("empty capture")

    # --- Power spectrum (Welch-like average) ---
    step = int(args.nfft * (1 - args.overlap))
    if step <= 0: step = args.nfft // 2
    n_frames = max(1, (len(x) - args.nfft) // step + 1)
    window = np.hanning(args.nfft).astype(np.float32)
    psd = np.zeros(args.nfft, dtype=np.float64)
    for i in range(n_frames):
        seg = x[i*step : i*step + args.nfft] * window
        psd += np.abs(np.fft.fftshift(np.fft.fft(seg)))**2
    psd /= n_frames
    psd_db = 10*np.log10(np.maximum(psd, 1e-20))

    freqs = args.fc + np.fft.fftshift(np.fft.fftfreq(args.nfft, d=1/args.fs))

    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(11,4))
    ax.plot(freqs/1e6, psd_db, lw=0.8)
    ax.set_xlabel("RF frequency (MHz)")
    ax.set_ylabel("Relative power (dB, uncalibrated)")
    ax.set_title(f"Power spectrum — fc={args.fc/1e6:.3f} MHz, fs={args.fs/1e6:.3f} MS/s, NFFT={args.nfft}, Hann, {int(args.overlap*100)}% overlap")
    ax.grid(alpha=.25)
    fig.tight_layout()
    fig.savefig(os.path.join(args.out, "spectrum.png"), dpi=160)
    plt.close(fig)

    # --- Waterfall (spectrogram) ---
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(11,5))
    ax.specgram(x, NFFT=args.nfft, Fs=args.fs, noverlap=int(args.nfft*args.overlap),
                cmap="magma", scale="dB")
    ax.set_xlabel("Elapsed time (s)")
    ax.set_ylabel("Baseband frequency (Hz, relative to center)")
    ax.set_title("Waterfall — relative dB, no absolute calibration")
    fig.tight_layout()
    fig.savefig(os.path.join(args.out, "waterfall.png"), dpi=160)
    plt.close(fig)

    meta = {
        "data": args.data, "fs": args.fs, "fc": args.fc, "fmt": args.fmt,
        "nfft": args.nfft, "window": "Hann", "overlap": args.overlap,
        "units": "relative dB (uncalibrated)",
        "n_samples": int(len(x)), "duration_s": len(x)/args.fs
    }
    with open(os.path.join(args.out, "analysis_meta.json"), "w") as f:
        json.dump(meta, f, indent=2)
    print(json.dumps(meta, indent=2))

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

S2.2 make_synthetic_tones.py — validation signal generator

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Generate complex I/Q with a +50 kHz tone and a −75 kHz tone 6 dB weaker.
Sample rate: 2.4 MS/s. Output: synthetic_tones.cf32 (complex64 little-endian).
Dependencies: numpy
"""
import numpy as np

fs = 2_400_000
duration = 2.0
t = np.arange(int(fs*duration)) / fs

# amplitudes: 1.0 and 10^(-6/20) = 0.501187
a1, a2 = 1.0, 10**(-6/20)
x = a1*np.exp(2j*np.pi*50_000*t) + a2*np.exp(2j*np.pi*(-75_000)*t)
x += 0.001*(np.random.randn(len(t)) + 1j*np.random.randn(len(t)))

x.astype(np.complex64).tofile("synthetic_tones.cf32")
print("wrote synthetic_tones.cf32", len(x), "samples")

S2.3 validate_peaks.py — pass/fail audit

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Audit the synthetic validation capture against the three pass criteria.
Dependencies: numpy
"""
import numpy as np

fs = 2_400_000
nfft = 4096
x = np.fromfile("synthetic_tones.cf32", dtype=np.complex64)
window = np.hanning(nfft)
seg = x[:nfft] * window
psd = np.abs(np.fft.fftshift(np.fft.fft(seg)))**2
psd_db = 10*np.log10(np.maximum(psd, 1e-20))
freqs = np.fft.fftshift(np.fft.fftfreq(nfft, d=1/fs))

def peak_near(f0, tol=2*fs/nfft):
    idx = np.where(np.abs(freqs - f0) <= tol)[0]
    return idx[np.argmax(psd_db[idx])]

i50 = peak_near(50_000)
i75 = peak_near(-75_000)
f50, f75 = freqs[i50], freqs[i75]
l50, l75 = psd_db[i50], psd_db[i75]

bin_width = fs/nfft
print(f"Bin width: {bin_width:.1f} Hz")
print(f"+50 kHz peak: {f50:.1f} Hz (expected 50000, Δ={f50-50000:.1f} Hz)")
print(f"−75 kHz peak: {f75:.1f} Hz (expected -75000, Δ={f75+75000:.1f} Hz)")
print(f"Relative level: {l50-l75:.2f} dB (expected 6.00 dB)")

ok_freq = abs(f50-50000) <= bin_width and abs(f75+75000) <= bin_width
ok_sign = f50 > 0 and f75 < 0
ok_level = abs((l50-l75) - 6.0) <= 1.0
print(f"Frequency location: {'PASS' if ok_freq else 'FAIL'}")
print(f"Frequency sign:     {'PASS' if ok_sign else 'FAIL'}")
print(f"Relative level:     {'PASS' if ok_level else 'FAIL'}")
print("OVERALL:", "PASS" if (ok_freq and ok_sign and ok_level) else "FAIL")

Supplementary Notes

Supplementary Note S4.1 — Why the 2017 findings are not generalized

The 2017 work examined a single Advanced Bionics HiRes 90K implant and reported Manchester coding, 12-bit words, 300-word packets, plaintext forward-link data, and no non-volatile RAM in the implanted receiver[reference:25]. Those findings were accurate for that device and that generation. They are not a specification for all cochlear implants, and they do not imply that a current device is unencrypted, lacks authentication, or is susceptible to over-the-air injection. The physical proximity of NFMI remains a strong architectural mitigation: the magnetic field falls off steeply, and a quarter-inch air gap is not a long-range channel[reference:26]. The correct scientific posture is to treat every device as UNKNOWN until measured, and to report a null result as a result.

Supplementary Note S4.2 — Regulatory and cybersecurity context

The FDA’s June 2025 final guidance, Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality System Considerations and Content of Premarket Submissions, updates the 2023 guidance and finalizes the March 2024 draft on Section 524B of the FD&C Act[reference:27]. It recommends cybersecurity device design, labeling, and documentation in premarket submissions for cyber devices. This context is cited to frame why a receive-only, non-destructive measurement workflow is the appropriate scientific default: active transmission to an implanted medical device is not a research method; it is an unauthorized intervention. A 2025 IoMT attack-taxonomy preprint is also noted as a general security-context reference, not as evidence about any specific device or person[reference:28].

Supplementary Note S4.3 — What the workflow can and cannot establish

Can establish: that a receiver, antenna, capture chain, metadata sidecar, hash, FFT mapping, waterfall time axis, and Manchester decoder are working correctly on known signals; that a candidate emission exists at a given frequency and time under documented receiver settings; that a captured bitstream is consistent with a particular line code or frame length if the decode is reproducible.

Cannot establish: the presence of an implant in a person; consent or lack of consent; the identity of any operator; causation of any reported experience; the security posture of a specific implanted device; or the truth of any allegation. Those require independent clinical examination, device forensics with lawful authority, and corroborated records.

Supplementary Note S4.4 — Formalized Boundary-Condition Advisor statement

The advisor position is: measure what can be measured, label what is inferred, preserve what is original, and refuse to let an extreme narrative overwrite a null result. This is the same discipline that separates a published physical-layer finding from a speculative claim, and it is the condition under which a supplementary methods document can be submission-ready at a Nature Portfolio journal.
