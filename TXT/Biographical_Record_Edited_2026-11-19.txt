Why this history is written down

This history is set down plainly because it has become necessary to explain who I am, and why I am telling it. For most of my life I was a person with perfectly healthy hearing going about his way: a musician and an audio engineer, a comeback kid, round four or whatever. Then, abruptly, came an implantation, a broadcast, and a commentary loop, all at once. What follows is the backstory that stands against the message of that loop. Whatever its source or intention, the loop's cumulative message has been unmistakable to me: that I am of no value, of no worth, of no humanity, of no dignity; unworthy of respect, and unworthy of the time I spend on this earth. That has been my sentiment, my experience, and my reality. It is a character assassination, whether advertent or inadvertent. This record is who I actually am.

The broadcast and the shielding

When the unexplained broadcast began, I poured a large share of my bandwidth into shielding my workspace. What started as a makeshift stand-up rig thrown together in an hour so I could get back to work became, over months of lost sleep, hours spent seaming six, seven, eight layers of material around a frame while an artificial voice narrated my every move. Eventually I set aside the career-defining passion project I had been driving, and I restarted two medications: my ADHD medication - the Puritan voice in the commentary had made a point of my "checkered" past - and my buprenorphine-naloxone, which restored some measure of focus. I am a musician and an audio engineer. Unexplained, intelligible sound that will not stop is not something you can ignore. For the first months I hoped it was some misfortunate criminal temporarily fixated on me, someone who would lose interest and move on. I told myself to keep my head down, not to engage, and to let it pass so I could stay on my own path - deep "checkered" past roots driving me to keep going, and they still do.

After I exhausted that hope, the threat stopped being something I could ignore and became a definite personal emergency. I accepted it as an emergency somewhere amid the materials purchases and the later geographical purchases, all of which are documented. The broadcast - Auracast or equivalent - carried intelligible, contrived suppositions that I treated as true until proven otherwise, including threats and references to anonymous people using "Task Rabbits" primarily to keep devices connected. That device-connectivity period is the first category of my financial-loss excursions, and it lines up directly with the onset of the intelligible subject matter in the transmissions.

Around that same time - anchored by the February call to local non-emergency services - I was also making electronics purchases at Amazon, Apple, and similar retailers. I have some computer background: an introduction to C++ around 2014, self-taught Pro Tools from my early professional experience, JMP statistical analyses in a scientist role, and MATLAB mechanistic modeling (with credit to BK's coding). I understand enough to run these tools and modify them, but that is the edge of my experience. I have no hacking experience whatsoever.

The record, stated plainly

To be clear about my "checkered" past: it is entirely personal, nonviolent substance use. Several petty theft convictions and a DUI before the age of 21 across three Tennessee counties, between roughly ages 18 and 20; a single petty theft charge without arrest in my current state around 2015; a second DUI in this state around 2017; and a pharmacy technician certification revoked over a CIII diversion, again personal substance use. All of it. The thefts themselves: meals, cough medicine, and in one case a university textbook resold for cash - all before age 21, all under a couple of hundred dollars. None of it is unrelated to my home situation through the teenage years, from about age 14 until I left Tennessee for Maryland at 21 or 22. For roughly seven years, things worsened in step with my then-separated parents.

My father was, until those events, a respectable community member - a well-regarded "architect," as I describe him, a "Master Carpenter" of all things residential. He was clerk and lead enlisted chief of the roughneck crews that spent six months at a time building JCPenney stores from New York down to Puerto Rico, and he met my mother in Macon, Georgia, before my memories begin, after she had left my would-be-rockstar biological father. The Weddington side, to my understanding, was all musicians - Billy Ray Cyrus country, Kentucky and West Virginia - one of them married to an Ashland, Kentucky bar owner, where the thing to do was play.

My biological father was a dreamer, and substance abuse followed him. My senior year of high school was only the second time I met him. The first was about a week in fifth grade, staying with my "Gran Gran" - a crazy, sweet old bat who adored me, her first grandchild, and was understandably sad to see my mother go. I tried Toaster Strudels for the first time, was getting into skateboarding, and spent one night at my father's studio apartment with his girlfriend; we went somewhere around Ashland and watched Goldmember, the third Austin Powers film. The second time I met him was my senior year, after his stroke - air duster, a 30-pack of Bud, and injected non-injectable oxymorphone extended release. I visited and caught up with my younger sister and brother, who then took care of him until my senior year of college (ironically), when he passed, February or March of 2018 if I remember correctly. That was my third trip to the Ashland, Kentucky-West Virginia region, again about a week.

My mother's father was the best man I ever knew: Master Chief Larry Alfeld, retired in 1979 if I remember right. He was fond of my "dad" - himself an enlisted master chief carpenter. They crossed paths after my mother's two-year Air Force stint, then a mall-manager job in Macon, with me riding along in a Ford Escort, a newly found love of Marlboros, bar nights with me in the belly, apparently, and that song - "Come to My Window... something, something." She found herself managing a mall (Air Force police and security roots, I think), then managing in Macon, Georgia, where she crossed paths with JCPenney's head enlisted site-construction man, Thomas "Tommy" Dennis Crosby II. My brother is the III, third-born, 1996; I am 1992, and ironically within 24 hours of Tommy's middle-younger brother's son Travis Crosby - September 1st and 2nd, 1992. Gas was about 91 cents a gallon, to paint the picture. Like my grandfather - nine months out, then home, then repeat, by way of the Navy - Tommy did six-month stints along the Eastern Seaboard, and for two or three years we followed: Puerto Rico for a stint, somewhere around Orlando for a stint, somewhere around New York City for a stint, and then we planted ourselves in Tommy's homeland, Bean Station, Tennessee.

The Crosby name has deep roots in agricultural engineering, including a press and mill still standing, preserved near Cades Cove outside Gatlinburg, Tennessee. Tommy's father and grandmother owned about thirty acres of worn-down mountain now edging Cherokee Lake, flooded in the 1940s by the TVA for Oak Ridge hydroelectric power. He bought a small half-acre plot from his father, with a mobile home and a jet ski, a Grateful Dead sticker on its nose - though I do not think he knew it as anything more than a badass lightning skull - the jet ski trailered behind a 1979 gold Trans Am, T-tops, you bet. My memories start on that plot, next to my "Papaw Dennis," who passed from melanoma around 2000. He was a quiet 6'3", two-hundred-something old farm hand, a hard worker with a fine barn, six-bay coverage, and a once-a-year molasses cook-off powered by a 1934 John Deere D tractor belt - the original power take-off drive, or so I figure; I am rusty on it. My memories start there, on the adjacent plot beneath the Baldys'.

The Baldy family lived above us. The father, John, was an engineer or equivalent, and he built from nothing the convention center, or its equivalent, that sits right off Exit 8 on I-40. His wife Mary was the artistic one; in their barn she fashioned these beautiful colored-glass pieces. Their son Evan was in my grade, as was Travis. The Baldy place is where I first saw a PC - a flight simulator, old school, you will understand. Their older son Paul - Paulie Baldy, a good guy who became an ENT - took a full driver backswing at me when I was about four and a half, five, or six. That one was my fault: I was book smart but, as they said, without a lick of common sense - occasionally a little Lord Fauntleroy, however you spell it, a bookworm, prompt, never a red bear pulled from the behavior chart, and two zeros with the smiley face, off the bus proudly before 4:30 p.m. for Dragon Ball Z. What happens next?

My father was out to sea and home on weekends. That meant something like: leave Friday, drive ten hours, and spend about eighteen hours at the lake and hanging out. Meanwhile he bought the piece above the Baldys' from my grandmother - part of that steadily dispersing thirty-acre sprawl, a dozen hilltops along the lake. A 22-year-old Tommy bought about four and a half acres running up from Lakeshore Road - eighteen miles of one-lane road following the TVA flood, twice the whole way - about the width of a small football pitch, rectangular, climbing past a small pond to a plateau covering a third of the parcel, hilltopped and overlooking Papaw Dennis's plot, and below it the weekend cove, courtesy of Oak Ridge hydroelectric. On weekends he used his dad's tractor or rented a Bobcat - the original zero-turn, with a bucket, mind you - and dug down to red clay for a basic A-frame he had penciled himself, designed around the time my memory begins, somewhere around New York City. My mom - partly, I suspect, to keep up with the Joneses - turned that sensible base A-frame (rectangle, then build up: basement, a floor, then a triangle and whatever rooms you can fit inside it, otherwise Griswold it) into something much larger and much harder to heat: an A-frame cut with another A-frame, a full wing, into the base.

They dug out the red clay, and from kindergarten on we lived on that plot below the Baldys'. Around second grade a trucker moved our mobile home up to the plateau, the build ongoing around it: cinder blocks in, walls coming up, and finally a steep green tin roof that was a pain in the ass to finish. Papaw Dennis was the connection that got him in with JCPenney, or at least how it came about; years before, as my dad entered his teens, he had opened a hardware store in Bean Station. Beer, reasonable THC percentages, 103.5 WIMZ with John-Boy and Billy in the morning, and the only five hundred songs you will ever need, still playing to this day - they just keep making the tower taller and more powerful so you can have that in Florida if you need it, or maybe as far as Atlanta.

Remember: Vicodin is the best parts of a beer with none of the consequences, and you can work longer. That is the hook, at least from my perspective and experience. My dad's addiction had become a weird cycle people just do not understand. It is a blur, but that must have been late 2011, and by March 2012 or so I had spent all my time figuring out how to get an opiate if I was lucky - or really, just get to a store for DXM, the dissociative. One day Dad and my brother were out on a mowing job. Dad had a couple of gigs and was trying, in his way, to instill work ethic in my brother, but the reality was that 98 percent of life was being sick - withdrawal, with little hour-long jobs to feel normal when nothing was. That is my clouded take. I blacked out, dehydrated, after riding a teen-sized BMX bike to Morristown and back for two bottles of cough medicine, three and a half miles down Lakeshore Drive. That is addiction - and a long way in a place where everyone has a car; it is the base necessity, no different than home. They were out with the mower and trailer, and before they got back I came to, having blacked out, and distinctly found that I must have fallen backward looking out over the front yard. Based on how I came to, had I fallen a hair to the right, I would have landed my head square on a log-splitting blade - heavy-sided, and not out of place, for your information. It scared me enough to immediately email my mother. She had a Navy buddy who got me a bed at St. Christopher's Inn in Garrison, New York. I spent about 120 days there, and it was a beautiful experience - among other adjectives - life experience, and so on.

I went to Schenectady, outside Albany, right next to Troy - Congress Street or Congress Avenue, I forget. I walked to Main and bused to Barnes & Noble, and within about the first month I stopped the 20 mg Vyvanse prescribed at baseline in treatment and started the dextromethorphan - not challenged, just still. I met a pot dealer and went from working the storefront to boxing in the back. House's DXM guy, ditto - though I am kidding; I think that character was a Good Will Hunting type, a physics prodigy or something. Anyway. I kept it together enough to save six hundred dollars. I trained down to D.C., Mom drove me halfway to my brother and dad to pick up the Protege - I had attempted a rebuild with my cousin but had not reassembled it with diligence, etcetera - a six-hundred-dollar mechanic's repair, a day's work. Like an addict, in the car with Mom driving me halfway, I texted an old buddy out of the blue and spent pretty much every dime on oxycodone IR the moment I arrived. Ashamed - but very understanding, and surprisingly, genuinely wanting me to succeed, Dad withdrew six hundred dollars, no questions asked, and I was on my way. That meant a lot.

But still. Still. My high-school sweetheart started texting me after I got back to New York, and one day, like something in the rut, I grabbed a stack of coffee-table books, my two duffel bags, my amp and guitar, an adjacent roommate's Korn bass - I will make it right, God willing - and left New York just like that. Four hours in, I dozed off, ran off the road, and the oil pan got shotgun-littered enough to leak a half-quart every thirty minutes or so. I made it to Tennessee and parked at a church near my sweetheart's place. Long story short: I round-tripped from her dad's front door - I never even stepped foot on the lawn - back to the church, about three football fields around a bend or two. Cops, pipe in the seat, car towed, minus two hundred dollars, do not pass go. That was quite literally about thirty hours into my New York exit.

But I was still halfway in on recovery, in the weirdest, most delusional way. I borrowed - and I do believe eventually repaid - a thousand dollars to get a studio in Knoxville, but of course the Barnes & Noble job I expected was not waiting for me when I arrived. About a year of petty theft followed: basic foods, beer. I enjoyed the apartment for three months, met someone, had a lot of fun for ten weeks or so - and then, in short, her father made a point of paying for an abortion. Painful, confusing, but par for the course at the time. I moved back to Dad's. He was shooting up to the point that I instinctively intervened: one day his arm was approaching gangrene, the worst I had seen, endocarditis the concern, etcetera.

Me, high and mighty - though clearly full of it, but with at least 125 days of sobriety to know it was possible - I offered to watch the disability check while he addressed the arm. I had talked to my brother beforehand, and he did not want to confront him. After some emotional back-and-forth, to my total surprise - my brother later told me it wrecked him royally - Dad had his loaded Ruger, or whatever his loaded handgun was, and in Tennessee that is something you know, not guess. He held it to my head, seriously, and asked me to leave, to put it one way, and I did. However many hours later I came to the door and apologized. That night, in the basement, I realized he might come in at any moment and shoot me - not something you think about until something like that happens. I stacked things against the door. A few hours passed, and I got the urgency to attempt something that fortunately - as real as it got - did not happen. I learned a lot from that experience about respecting another person's life: it is no one's place to take one, and fortunately that did not happen, either way.

But within the same day I approached the neighbors - who now lived where the Baldys' used to - over the fence. On the tail end of DXM, but with the realization that it was time to end the operation at my home by law, a clear dead end - even in panic and unusualness, not unclearly - I saw the neighbors pull in, head down, within earshot, still about sixty feet away. I motioned and explained: call the cops, please - my mother had turned my phone off a week before, for good reason at the time, I do not remember. I immediately took a shower, dressed for church, and told the truth about my house: a grow room in the basement, a known pill mill, and a man about to lose his arm. Whether they knew or not - my father then exercised his right not to let them search, despite them pushing for several minutes. What I eventually surmise happened: once I reported, they placed me cooperatively in the car - the neighbors naturally must have said something was going on, overall. I watched from the car for what felt like ten minutes, and then I went to jail, and then to Knox County on a warrant.

He had exercised his rights and said I was high and growing weed in locations off his property - pointing randomly, I recall, not knowing why. That was why, and it was a lie, and I needed him to pick me up in Knoxville. A couple of weeks later, I think: about a week and change at the local Grainger County jail, and then, just when I thought I was leaving, they put me in a Knox County car - a hell of an experience, but decisions. The warrant was for a petty theft: a bottle of cough syrup at a CVS outside UT, where an associate confronted me and I identified myself enough, then left, and ended up with a warrant unbeknownst to me. Anyway - off to Knox. Dad and my brother picked me up. I got in the back seat and Dad tossed a Grainger Today at me: I was on the front page. And maybe two years before that, I had been in that same paper as a promising kid headed toward big things - and a musician, etcetera, some quarterly spotlight feature. The contrast.

For approximately the next year I became proficient at obtaining groceries and beer from select retailers, doing it for my brother and me with the car that worked well enough. That was life. My father's addiction was full-blown: every dime except the house payment. It came to a head at two in the morning, or some random time: my brother, the ENTs, the cops. Long story short, he was hospitalized - hepatitis B. My brother and I, a duffel bag each - me with the guitar, him with his Xbox - got on a Greyhound in January or February of 2014 to Maryland. My aunt, my mother, my cousin Maggie, my cousin Zach, and his dog all pitched in, and everyone moved in together on Rayfield Drive, Germantown, Maryland.

I got a night job at Rite Aid within the first month - though, from a distance, I sold my drums on eBay for $320, split with my brother (the shells were a split Christmas deal). That was years of stuff, sold for about $312, and I spent it in ten days on alcohol. But I did get hired at Rite Aid immediately, overnight, by Fatima, working with a guy named Herb. I tried to steal from Safeway - chicken and dumplings, egg noodles, green beans, chicken broth - in a basket, to cook dinner for the house, to do something nice. This must have been within a week or two of getting here. No arrest, but a ticket, on record - though taken care of, even still. Rite Aid overnight, then shift supervisor during the day. One night - I do not know what the hell, or why - a fifty-something Asian woman, who appeared high on cough medicine at the time, roamed the store for hours. In hindsight, I think she was homeless and staying warm through a hard winter, just pretending to shop from 1 a.m. to 4 a.m. or whatever. That was the last time I did cough medicine - I had taken some at the start of that shift, and, I do not know, it was a weird thing. But I got onto day shift as a supervisor.

I went to Montgomery College and took the entrance exam. Initially, computer science - the family's foot-in-the-door logic, hence the C++ course - but as soon as I took a biology course with Sean Cooney, if I remember the name right, I started to enjoy learning again. The rest, I think, I have discussed. This is yet more background, apparently necessary to explain how I now maintain - well, perfectly productive on multiple levels - on a very small dose of Suboxone, which I know curbs alcohol cravings. Between then and now I hit an all-time low with alcohol and benzodiazepines while working as a pharmacy technician - the certification had been a good move for U.S. medical-school applications - and then a CIII diversion, self-disclosed, in December 2017. I fell into a depression. I do not know what happened with the charges - I think they may have been dropped; I recall multiple CII listings on a letter I received in January 2018, accessible only to the pharmacist, but that is a separate situation.

Benzodiazepine withdrawal, alcohol as the remedy, an acetaminophen-ethanol harm scare, and an observation commitment in August 2018, preceded by a DUI - my first in this state, but my second overall, at nineteen or twenty and again at twenty-six or twenty-seven. From then to December 2019: probation, plus addressing a 2013 warrant from Hamblen County, Tennessee, which flagged high on background checks, all while working two jobs. Despite the bullshit - after the pharmacy-certification revocation in January 2018, despite the MCAT and fair standing - that path was precluded, realistically and appropriately, I think. Buca di Beppo plus Amazon shopper, eighty-plus hours a week, an Intoxalock, and finally a job in my degree field (in Frederick, twenty miles away - had to have a car) finally curbed the alcohol. I have described everything from December 2019 to December 2024 in high detail elsewhere.

All of the above is to explain, to folks who see someone taking a dose of buprenorphine and Adderall - orally, about five seconds, sixty seconds total in a day - that it is medicine that helps. There is ample, ample, ample evidence from 2020 to the present that I am a strong, contributing, productive member of society: a purple belt in BJJ; a commendably professional EP of music, written and engineered by me across instruments - a testament, like BJJ, to something good; and the work, also described endlessly elsewhere. It sucks to even have to do this - that is how my ego works - but here I am. The point is: I take roughly 0.5 mg of buprenorphine sublingually and an equally low dose of ADHD medication, relative to the population, with prescriptions on record for both and ample indication and history. I request that anyone who wishes to point to these maintenance medications point instead to the specific actions, behaviors, and indications that have occurred outside the privacy of my home. They are not hidden because they are shameful; they are private because of bias and shortsightedness - and in my case, a substantial overreach into personal, private health information.

Why the history matters

This is why the backstory matters. I did not write it to excuse a single thing; I wrote it so the record reflects the whole person. A musician and an audio engineer with ordinary, healthy hearing, going about his way, hit bottom more than once and got back up - the comeback kid, round four - when, abruptly, an implantation, a broadcast, and a commentary loop arrived together. Whatever their source or intention, their cumulative message has been unmistakable: that I am of no value, of no worth, of no humanity, of no dignity; unworthy of respect, and unworthy of the time I spend on this earth. That has been my sentiment, the experience, and the reality I have lived under - a character assassination, whether advertent or inadvertent. It is also false. The record above is the evidence: the work, the sobriety, the purple belt, the music, and the people who kept believing in me. I am not nothing. No one is.
